## Medical framing

The described target is the abductor hallucis. The supplied concept has three positions: the medial side of the foot, plantar to the first MTP joint, and dorsal to that joint. This is an explanatory schematic, not a validated fitting or stimulation protocol. The user clarified that the textile is an open sleeve with heel anchoring and a big-toe opening, not a full sock.

The cited randomized trial studied 28 women (48 feet) for one month, comparing orthoses/exercise with the same care plus NMES. It did not demonstrate additional benefit in primary outcomes from added stimulation. It did not test Albea or establish primary prevention. Consequently, the demo presents prevention as a research ambition, not a proven consumer benefit. No treatment protocol, pain-relief guarantee, correction claim, regulatory authorization or medical-device classification is invented.

## France and United States

English and French versions are complete and retain the same journey when switching language. The target is 2027 in both markets, subject to development and applicable requirements. Demo pricing is €150 in both languages, explicitly illustrative in EUR; actual US pricing, taxes, fulfillment, sizing and launch authorizations remain undecided.

## Content authenticity

- No fabricated press endorsements, founder identity or verified-customer ratings.
- Quotations are fictional persona perspectives, individually labeled and explained beside the section.
- Images are original AI-generated concept visuals, not finished product photography or photographs of the surgeon.
- All shopping actions are mock operations. Checkout never sends an order or payment.
- The sign-up form validates and simulates confirmation entirely on the page. It neither stores nor transmits the email address.

## Recommended follow-up discovery

Interview people in each group in both countries; evaluate clarity of the open-sleeve concept, comfort concerns, setup expectations, price sensitivity and language. Use early-sign and family-history groups as initial research priorities, with standing professionals next. These priorities are product hypotheses rather than evidence of demand.
